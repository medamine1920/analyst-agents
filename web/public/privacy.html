How your data is handled

Analyst Console is a beta. This page says plainly what it keeps, where it goes and how to remove it.

What is stored

  • Your account: the name you chose; your password only as a one-way hash (it cannot be read back); your sign-in sessions; and 90 days of account activity (sign-ins, with the address and browser they came from), which you can see in Settings.
  • Your questions and answers, with the numbers computed for them, so you can open them again from your history.
  • Data you add: uploaded files, and the metrics generated from them. A connected database is read when you ask, never written to.
  • Credentials: database passwords, tokens for connected tools and your own model keys are stored encrypted, and are only ever shown masked.
  • Feedback you give on answers.

Who can see it

Your data sources, connections, keys and answers are private to your account. Other testers cannot see them. The console's administrator can see the list of accounts, the feedback testers give, and which questions were asked (for running and improving the service).

What leaves the server

  • To the AI model: to answer a question, the question, the names and definitions of the metrics, and the numbers computed for it are sent to the model provider: Groq by default, or the provider whose key you added (Anthropic, OpenAI or Google). Their own terms then apply. Your raw files are not sent; only the results of the queries the answer needs.
  • To tools you connect: only what you send there (an export to Slack, Teams or a Google Sheet, for example).
  • Nothing is sold, shared for advertising, or used by this console to train models.

Google Drive and Sheets

If you connect Google, the console can read only the files you choose in Google's file picker and the Sheets it creates when you export an answer. It does not browse the rest of your Drive. Its use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Disconnecting Google in the console deletes the stored access and withdraws it at Google; you can also remove it at any time from your Google account's third-party access page.

Removing your data

  • Remove a data source (and its files and metrics) under Data sources.
  • Disconnect a tool under Connections; remove a model key under Settings → API keys.
  • To delete your account and everything linked to it, ask the person who invited you (the console's administrator).

Security

Sign-in is required for everything; you can turn on two-step sign-in and sign out other sessions in Settings. Connections to outside services go only to public addresses, with time and size limits.
